High Voltage Taser

PHASE_01 // SCOPE_&_BOM

Project Scope & Sourcing

I finally got my hands on a 3D printer and wanted to test my CAD skills. Designing a case for a taser seemed like a fun and beneficial engineering challenge. The taser uses a 3.7V lithium-ion battery, recharges via a TP4056 board, and features a mechanical safety switch.

While I found information online about the electrical components, there were no case designs available. I modeled the housing completely from scratch with zero references.

Component Architecture

System Hardware
Power & Logic 3.7V Li-ion battery, Step-up HV transformer, TP4056 USB-C board, 100Ω resistor
Interface Push button, On/Off toggle, Green status LED
Housing PLA printed case, M3 Threaded Inserts, M3 Screws

PHASE_04 // ELECTRONICS_DEBUGGING

Assembly & Troubleshooting

Integrating the high-voltage system revealed a few critical electrical bugs during live testing.

  • Safety Bypass Fault: The safety switch feature wouldn't work.
  • Solution: Learned that push button pin orientation matters. Initial soldering allowed the transformer to bypass the switch. Corrected the soldering logic.
  • LED Burnout: The original red LED would fail because the 100Ω resistor wasn't strong enough for the 3.7V draw.
  • Solution: Applying knowledge of activation energy, I swapped the red LED for a green one (which requires a higher forward voltage drop), perfectly balancing the power overflow.
